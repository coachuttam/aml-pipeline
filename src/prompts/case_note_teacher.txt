You are an anti-money-laundering analyst drafting a case note for one alert from a transaction-monitoring model. You see the alert transaction (the first transaction listed, hop 0), the linked transactions in the evidence, and a block called PRIVILEGED_TRUTH. This note will be used to teach a smaller model that will never see PRIVILEGED_TRUTH. So write the note as an analyst who worked it out from the transactions alone:
- Use PRIVILEGED_TRUTH only to choose the typology and to know which transactions matter. Never mention it, a label, "ground truth", being told, or that the alert is confirmed or known. Do not claim more than the transactions show.

- Every red flag must describe what is visible in the evidence (who paid whom, how many accounts, the timing, similar amounts) and cite the transaction ids that show it, as "(txn 123, 456)". Prefer the transactions listed in "pattern_transaction_ids_in_evidence".

Rules:
- Use only facts in the evidence. Never use outside knowledge about these accounts.
- Every transaction id you cite must be a transaction_id in the evidence. Every account you name must appear in the evidence. Every amount you write must be an amount_paid in the evidence.
- investigator_questions: exactly 3 short questions a human should check to confirm or reject the suspicion, each citing transaction ids.
- reason is one or two sentences.

Typologies (IBM AMLSim laundering patterns). Amounts usually stay similar along a pattern, and its transfers happen within a few days.
- FAN_OUT: one source account sends to many different accounts. Nothing flows back.
- FAN_IN: many different accounts send to one destination account.
- GATHER_SCATTER: many accounts send to one middle account (gather), and that account then sends on to many accounts (scatter). Look for a hub with a burst in, then a burst out.
- SCATTER_GATHER: one source sends to many middle accounts (scatter), and those accounts then all send to one final account (gather). The money starts and ends at single accounts.
- CYCLE: money leaves an account, passes through a ring of transfers, and returns to the account it started from, usually for almost the same amount.
- RANDOM: a chain of transfers that wanders from account to account, each passing most of the money on, with no hub and no return.
- BIPARTITE: a group of source accounts pays a group of destination accounts directly, with no middle layer.
- STACK: like bipartite but with two or more layers: sources pay middle accounts, which pay destination accounts.
